\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textbf{Q1}] \textbf{(D) all of the above.} Horizontal microprogramming dedicates one bit per control signal, so each bit directly \emph{is} the signal (no decoder needed), which is exactly why the microinstruction word is wider than the encoded-field vertical alternative. See the provenance note in the questions file for this answer's verification status.

\item[\textbf{Q2}] \textbf{(A).} The 26-bit microinstruction splits as $13 + X + Y$. The MUX has 8 status-bit inputs, so $Y = \lceil \log_2 8 \rceil = 3$. Hence $X = 26 - 13 - 3 = 10$ bits. The next-address field addresses control memory, so the number of words is $2^{10} = 1024$.

\item[\textbf{Q3}] \textbf{(C).} With at most two of 25 signals active, encode the (up to) two active signals in two independent fields, each needing $\lceil \log_2 25 \rceil = 5$ bits: $5 + 5 = 10$ bits. (A 2-bit field could not address 25 signals; 25 and 12 are not minimal.)

\item[\textbf{Q4}] \textbf{(C).} Field (a) can take one of four states (none or one of 3), needing $\lceil \log_2 4 \rceil = 2$ bits. Field (b) must allow any subset of six micro-operations (each on/off), needing 6 bits. Total $= 2 + 6 = 8$ bits.

\item[\textbf{Q5}] \textbf{(C).} Vertical encoding groups mutually-exclusive signals into short encoded fields, which must be expanded by a decoder before the datapath sees valid one-hot signals --- that decode step is exactly the extra delay vertical encoding pays for its narrower control word.

\item[\textbf{Q6}] \textbf{(B) Mapping unit.} The mapping unit converts the opcode in \texttt{IR} into the starting microaddress of that instruction's microprogram and loads it into \texttt{MPC}; control memory only supplies control words \emph{after} \texttt{MPC} already points at the right row.

\item[\textbf{Q7}] \textbf{(B).} Hardwired control has no memory-read delay at all (pure gates); horizontal microprogramming reads a wide control word directly with no decode step; vertical microprogramming pays an additional decoder delay on top of the memory read, making it the slowest of the three.
\end{enumerate}
