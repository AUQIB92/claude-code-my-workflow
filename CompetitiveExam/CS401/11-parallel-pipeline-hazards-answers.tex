\begin{sloppypar}
\noindent Provenance is repeated for auditability. Q1 and Q2 are verified real
PYQs from the local GATEOverflow compilation; Q3--Q8 are original GATE-pattern
questions. The answers below were independently derived from each stem. The
pipeline schedule explicitly marks the load-use bubble and the forwarding path.
\end{sloppypar}

\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textbf{Q1}] \textit{[GATE CSE 2002 Q2.6, verified real PYQ ---
GATEOverflow V3 p.81]} \textbf{(D).} Unequal stage delays limit the clock to
the slowest stage; data dependencies create data hazards; and shared hardware
can create structural hazards. Each can reduce pipelined performance.

\item[\textbf{Q2}] \textit{[GATE CSE 2008 Q76, verified real PYQ ---
GATEOverflow V3 p.83]} \textbf{(A).} A delayed branch defines a delay slot:
the instruction immediately following the branch in program memory executes
regardless of whether the branch is taken. The instruction is selected before
the branch outcome is known.

\item[\textbf{Q3}] \textit{[Original, GATE-pattern]} \textbf{10 cycles,
speedup $3.00$.} For $k=5$ stages and $n=6$ instructions,
$T_{\mathrm{pipe}}=k+(n-1)=5+5=10$. The non-pipelined time is $nk=30$,
so the finite-stream speedup is $30/10=3.00$.

\item[\textbf{Q4}] \textit{[Original, GATE-pattern]} \textbf{cycle 8,
1 bubble.} The schedule is:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c|cccccccc}
\textbf{Instruction} & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 & 6 & 7 & 8 \\
\hline
\texttt{LW}  & IF & ID & EX & MEM & WB & & & \\
\texttt{ADD} & & IF & ID & stall & EX & MEM & WB & \\
\texttt{SUB} & & & IF & stall & ID & EX & MEM & WB
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The load value arrives after MEM, so the immediately following ADD needs one
bubble even with forwarding. The ADD result is then forwarded to SUB, so no
second bubble is needed.

\item[\textbf{Q5}] \textit{[Original, GATE-pattern]} \textbf{(A).} One
instruction operates on many data elements, which is SIMD. Independent cores
run independent instruction streams on their tiles, which is MIMD.

\item[\textbf{Q6}] \textit{[Original, GATE-pattern]} \textbf{(B).} Two
stages demand one unavailable resource at the same time, so this is a
structural hazard. Splitting instruction and data memories permits both
accesses to proceed; stalling is another valid remedy.

\item[\textbf{Q7}] \textit{[Original, GATE-pattern]} \textbf{0.03 CPI.}
The expected contribution is
\[
  \Delta\mathrm{CPI}=f_{br}p_{miss}P_{flush}
  =0.20\times0.05\times3=0.03.
\]

\item[\textbf{Q8}] \textit{[Original, GATE-pattern]} \textbf{(A) and (C).}
Forwarding removes many ALU-to-ALU RAW stalls, but a load-use value can arrive
too late and still requires a bubble. A misprediction invalidates younger
wrong-path instructions. Register renaming removes false WAR/WAW name
conflicts, not a true RAW dependence on a value that has not been produced.
\end{enumerate}
