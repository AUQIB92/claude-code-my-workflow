## Structural observation (outside the formal 10, relevant to this medium)

Six emoji-led bullets in a row (📚📝✅🎯📊🌐, one icon per line, each a short noun-phrase fragment) is one of the most recognizable "AI wrote my LinkedIn post" signatures — more so than any single lexical tell. Consider collapsing to 3-4 items or dropping icons on at least half of them.

## What's working (don't touch)

- No cliché lexicon ("delve," "leverage," "robust framework," "navigate the complexities").
- No hedging stacks, no boilerplate transitions, no "not only X but also Y."
- The Pedro Sant'Anna credit line ("The infrastructure is entirely his; I just pointed it at undergrad CS") is self-effacing, specific, and the opposite of AI sycophancy — the strongest human-voice signal in the post.
- Concrete specifics (CS401, "hardwired control," "claude-code-my-workflow") read as genuine, not generic.

## Recommendation

Does not read as fully AI-drafted — specificity and the credit line carry real voice. But the opening hook, the emoji-bullet list, and the doubled tricolon/em-dash pattern are template-shaped and would be the first thing a LinkedIn-savvy reader's eye catches.

**Single biggest tell to fix first:** the line-1 opener ("What if X — so you could Y?" pattern). Cutting or flattening it does more for credibility than the smaller em-dash/tricolon fixes combined.
