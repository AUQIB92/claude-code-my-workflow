\section{Conceptual}

\textbf{Q1.} The true exponent of a floating-point number can be negative, and IEEE 754 stores it \emph{biased} ($E_{\text{stored}} = E_{\text{true}} + 127$ for single precision) rather than in two's complement. Explain why the bias is used: what property of ordering the biased representation gives the hardware, and why that matters for comparison instructions. Your answer must state what a stored exponent of all zeros and all ones is \emph{reserved} for.

\textbf{Q2.} A student writes a loop that accumulates $x = x + 0.1$ ten times and then tests $x = 1.0$. Explain why this test can fail even though $0.1 \times 10 = 1.0$ in exact arithmetic, and state the correct way to compare two floating-point numbers for ``equality.''

\section{Numerical}

\textbf{Q3.} Encode the value $7.75$ in IEEE 754 single precision (32 bits), showing: the binary expansion, the normalized scientific form $1.xxx_2 \times 2^E$, the sign bit, the biased exponent, and the 23-bit mantissa field. Write the final answer as an 8-hex-digit value (e.g.\ \texttt{0x412C0000}).

\textbf{Q4.} Decode the 32-bit pattern \texttt{0x3F400000} into its decimal value, showing each field: sign, exponent, mantissa. Check your work by confirming the value against its binary scientific form. (Recall the lecture's anchor: \texttt{0x3F800000} $= 1.0$.)

\section{Design}

\textbf{Q5.} Perform the single-precision addition $10.5 + 0.25$ qualitatively following the four-step add algorithm (align, add, normalize, round). For each step state precisely what happens to the mantissas and exponents. Then repeat for $1\,000\,000.0 + 0.25$, and explain, in terms of the alignment step, why the $0.25$ may be lost entirely --- the same effect the lecture demonstrated with $0.1$.

\textbf{Q6.} Derive the single-precision product of $1.5 \times 2.0$ using the multiplication rule (multiply mantissas, add exponents, subtract the bias once, normalize, round). State the exact product's sign, exponent, and mantissa fields, and show that the result re-encodes to exactly $3.0$. Explain why multiplication needs no alignment step.
